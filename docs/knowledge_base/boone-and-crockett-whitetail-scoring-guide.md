# Boone and Crockett Whitetail Scoring Guide

## Overview
This guide explains how Boone and Crockett (B&C) scoring works for **whitetail deer**. It is written for educational and retrieval use, not as a substitute for an official Boone and Crockett score chart or a certified Official Measurer.

For Iron Stag, this topic matters because the app stores an **unofficial, manual B&C estimate** for harvested bucks only. The app does not measure antlers from photos and does not certify a trophy for the Boone and Crockett records program.

## Key Information
- Boone and Crockett scoring is based on **antler measurements in inches**, usually recorded to the nearest **eighth of an inch**.
- The score is built from **inside spread credit**, **main beam length**, **normal point lengths**, and **mass measurements**.
- **Symmetry matters** for a typical score. Differences between the right and left antlers are deducted.
- **Abnormal points are treated differently** in typical and non-typical scoring.
- The official Boone and Crockett records process requires a **60-day drying period** before an official measurement can be taken.
- An unofficial or "green" score is useful for estimation, but it is not the same as an official record-book measurement.

## What Gets Measured
For a whitetail rack, the main categories are:

### 1. Inside Spread of Main Beams
This is the greatest inside distance between the two main beams. Boone and Crockett uses this as the spread credit, but the spread credit cannot exceed the length of the **longer main beam**.

### 2. Main Beams
Each main beam is measured from the burr along the outer curve to the beam tip.

### 3. Normal Points
These are the standard tine lengths on each side, usually labeled:
- `G-1` for brow tine
- `G-2`
- `G-3`
- `G-4`
- Additional normal points if the rack structure supports them

Each point is measured from its tip to the nearest edge of the main beam along the center line of the tine.

### 4. Circumferences or Mass Measurements
These are labeled `H-1` through `H-4` on each antler.
- `H-1`: smallest circumference between the burr and `G-1`
- `H-2`: smallest circumference between `G-1` and `G-2`
- `H-3`: smallest circumference between `G-2` and `G-3`
- `H-4`: smallest circumference between `G-3` and `G-4`

If an antler does not have enough points to place all four circumference locations normally, the measurer uses the standard Boone and Crockett chart instructions to place the remaining mass measurements.

### 5. Abnormal Points
These are points that do not match the normal typical frame. A point may be considered abnormal because of where it originates, how it branches, or how it projects relative to the normal frame.

## Measurements That Are Recorded but Not Usually Added to the Final Score
Boone and Crockett score charts also record:
- tip-to-tip spread
- greatest spread

These measurements are useful as descriptive data, but for whitetail scoring the actual spread credit used in the formula is the **inside spread of main beams**, capped by the longer main beam.

## How a Typical Whitetail Score Works
For a **typical whitetail**, the goal is to score the normal frame and subtract anything that breaks symmetry or falls outside that frame.

### Typical Gross Score
The starting gross typical score is:

`inside spread credit + right antler total + left antler total`

Where each antler total includes:
- main beam
- all normal points counted for that side
- all required circumference measurements

### Typical Deductions
From that subtotal, deductions are taken for:
- differences between paired measurements on the right and left sides
- total abnormal point length

Paired differences are calculated for matching items such as:
- right main beam vs left main beam
- right `G-1` vs left `G-1`
- right `G-2` vs left `G-2`
- right `H-1` vs left `H-1`
- and so on

The bigger the mismatch between the two antlers, the larger the deduction.

### Typical Net Score
The net typical score is:

`inside spread credit + both antler totals - side-to-side differences - abnormal point length`

In plain terms:
1. Add the spread credit.
2. Add all normal-frame measurements from both antlers.
3. Subtract lack of symmetry.
4. Subtract abnormal points.

That final result is the **net typical score**.

## How a Non-Typical Whitetail Score Works
For a **non-typical whitetail**, the process still starts with the normal frame, but abnormal points help the final score instead of hurting it.

### Non-Typical Base Score
First, calculate the normal-frame subtotal the same general way:

`inside spread credit + right antler normal measurements + left antler normal measurements`

Then subtract side-to-side differences in the matched normal measurements.

### Adding Abnormal Points
After the symmetry deductions are taken, abnormal point length is **added back in** for the non-typical score.

### Non-Typical Final Score
The simplified formula is:

`inside spread credit + both antler normal totals - side-to-side differences + abnormal point length`

That final result is the **net non-typical score**.

## Typical vs Non-Typical: The Core Difference
- **Typical score:** abnormal points are a deduction.
- **Non-typical score:** abnormal points are added to the final score.
- **Both systems:** differences between matched left and right normal measurements are deducted.

## Why Symmetry Matters So Much
Boone and Crockett is not just measuring size. It is also measuring how cleanly the antlers match a typical frame.

Two bucks can have similar gross inches, but the more symmetrical rack usually keeps more of its score in the typical category. A rack with many stickers, drop tines, kickers, or uneven paired measurements may lose typical inches through deductions but gain them back if entered as non-typical.

## Common Terms Hunters See on a Score Sheet
- **Gross score:** the total before all deductions are applied.
- **Net score:** the final score after deductions and category rules are applied.
- **Green score:** an unofficial score taken before the official drying-period requirement is complete.
- **Official score:** a measurement taken after the required drying period by a Boone and Crockett Official Measurer.
- **Awards minimum:** the minimum score needed for Boone and Crockett's Awards recognition.
- **All-time minimum:** the minimum score needed for the all-time records category.

## Official Drying Period
Under Boone and Crockett records-program policy, official measurements cannot be taken until the trophy has air-dried at normal room temperature for **at least 60 days** after the animal was killed, or after thawing/cleaning if freezing or certain processing occurred.

That means a buck can have:
- an early field estimate
- an unofficial green score
- a later official score after drying

Those numbers may not be identical because antlers can shrink slightly as they dry.

## Practical Step-by-Step
1. Confirm the buck is being scored as **typical** or **non-typical**.
2. Count points on each antler.
3. Record tip-to-tip spread and greatest spread for reference.
4. Measure the **inside spread of main beams**.
5. Measure the **main beam** on the right antler and on the left antler.
6. Measure every normal point (`G` measurements) on both sides.
7. Measure all required circumference locations (`H` measurements) on both sides.
8. Measure and list every abnormal point.
9. Add the normal-frame measurements to get the subtotal.
10. Calculate differences between matching right and left measurements.
11. For a typical score, subtract paired differences and abnormal points.
12. For a non-typical score, subtract paired differences and then add abnormal points.

## Simple Example
If a rack has:
- strong beam length
- long normal tines
- solid mass
- good inside spread
- very little asymmetry

it will usually score well as a typical buck.

If a rack has:
- a strong normal frame
- several stickers, drop tines, or split tines
- noticeable asymmetry

it may score lower as a typical buck, but better as a non-typical buck because those abnormal inches can be added back.

## Common Questions
**Q: What is the most important spread measurement?**  
A: The key spread value for the formula is the **inside spread of main beams**, not tip-to-tip spread or greatest spread.

**Q: Why can a huge rack lose so many inches as a typical?**  
A: Typical scoring penalizes asymmetry and subtracts abnormal points.

**Q: Why can the same buck score better as non-typical?**  
A: Non-typical scoring still deducts left-right differences, but it adds abnormal point length instead of subtracting it.

**Q: Is a green score official?**  
A: No. It is an unofficial estimate taken before the official drying-period requirement is satisfied.

**Q: Does Boone and Crockett require an official measurer?**  
A: Yes, if the trophy is being entered for official records-book consideration.
